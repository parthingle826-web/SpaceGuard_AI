## Abstract
Modern Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites generate continuous multi-subsystem telemetry spanning electrical power subsystems (EPS), thermal control subsystems (TCS), telemetry tracking and command (TT&C), and command and data handling (C&DH). Rapid orbital environmental cycles—specifically alternating eclipse and sunlit phases—create non-linear telemetry dynamics that challenge conventional out-of-limits threshold monitors, causing elevated false alarm rates and delayed anomaly isolation. In this work, we present **SpaceGuard AI**, an end-to-end telemetry anomaly detection and operator decision-support framework. SpaceGuard AI ingests 11 core telemetry parameters, applies domain-guided feature engineering (including EPS power product, thermal gradients, and nominal baseline deviations), and benchmarks three diverse machine learning classifiers: Decision Trees (CART), Support Vector Machines (SVM with RBF kernel), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost). Evaluated on an empirical test split of 1,440 telemetry observations across 8 distinct operational classes (Normal and 7 failure modes), the models achieved exceptional classification accuracy: SVM (99.44%, F1: 99.44%), XGBoost (99.03%, F1: 99.01%), and Decision Tree (98.12%, F1: 98.09%), with sub-millisecond inference latencies (0.0009 ms to 0.0259 ms per record). To support human-in-the-loop ground station operators, SpaceGuard AI couples model predictions with feature attribution explainability and an advisory rule engine that generates actionable recovery guidance.

**Keywords:** Spacecraft Health Monitoring, Telemetry Anomaly Detection, Machine Learning, Decision Tree, Support Vector Machine, XGBoost, Decision Support System, LEO Orbit Simulation.

## 1. Introduction
Satellite health monitoring is a mission-critical responsibility of space ground control stations. Telemetry downlinked from spacecraft serves as the sole window through which ground engineers ascertain the status of onboard electrical, thermal, structural, and communication subsystems. A modern satellite may transmit hundreds to thousands of parameter channels at frequencies ranging from 0.1 Hz to 10 Hz.

Traditional monitoring architectures rely heavily on static Out-Of-Limits (OOFL) alarms, where an alert is triggered whenever a parameter exceeds predefined static thresholds. However, spacecraft operating in Low Earth Orbit (LEO) experience orbital periods of approximately 90 to 100 minutes, encountering cyclic fluctuations in solar array illumination, battery charge/discharge cycles, and intense thermal gradients between sunlit passes and orbital eclipse. Under these cyclic conditions, static thresholds either produce high false alarm rates or must be widened so broadly that they fail to detect early onset degradations, gradual sensor drift, or compound anomalies spanning multiple subsystems.

Recent advances in machine learning (ML) offer promising solutions by learning complex, non-linear correlations across multi-channel telemetry. However, practical deployment in space operations requires addressing three key challenges:
1. **Multi-Model Comparative Performance:** Identifying trade-offs between linear/margin classifiers, tree ensembles, and inference latency.
2. **Explainability:** Providing flight controllers with transparent indicators of which telemetry features drove an anomaly alert.
3. **Decision Support:** Translating raw algorithmic classifications into actionable, rule-based recovery checklists without usurping operator command authority.

SpaceGuard AI was developed to address these operational requirements within a rigorous academic framework.

## 2. Literature Survey
Anomaly detection in satellite operations has evolved through three distinct technological generations:

1. **Limit Checking and Rule-Based Expert Systems:** Early spacecraft systems, such as NASA's Space Shuttle and early commercial communications satellites, utilized rule-based expert systems and static red/yellow threshold limits. While computationally simple, these systems cannot capture inter-parameter correlations (e.g., $P \approx V \times I$) and suffer from alarm fatigue during orbital mode transitions.
2. **Parametric & Time-Series Models:** Statistical approaches such as Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) and Kalman filtering model time-series trends. While effective for stationary signals, they struggle with abrupt, multi-channel regime changes such as eclipse entry or payload burst operations.
3. **Machine Learning & Deep Learning Approaches:**
   - *Hundman et al. (NASA JPL, 2018)* proposed Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks with non-parametric dynamic thresholding for telemetry anomaly detection on the Soil Moisture Active Passive (SMAP) and Curiosity rover missions. While effective, recurrent networks incur high computational overhead and lack inherent feature explainability.
   - *Yairi et al. (JAXA, 2017)* demonstrated data-driven health monitoring combining probabilistic clustering and kernel methods for telemetry degradation prognosis.
   - *Chen & Guestrin (2016)* introduced XGBoost, demonstrating superior performance on structured tabular data with second-order gradient optimization and strong generalization resilience against noisy observations.

SpaceGuard AI builds upon this body of work by conducting a systematic comparative analysis of tree-based, margin-based, and gradient-boosted architectures on multivariate spacecraft telemetry while preserving deterministic feature explainability and human-in-the-loop decision support.

## 3. Problem Statement & Objectives

### Problem Statement
Given an incoming multivariate satellite telemetry observation $\mathbf{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^D$ sampled at time $t$, design a reliable, low-latency machine learning system that:
1. Classifies the observation into either Normal state or one of 7 specific subsystem anomaly categories.
2. Assesses severity level $\mathcal{S} \in \{\text{Normal}, \text{Warning}, \text{Critical}\}$.
3. Quantifies individual parameter contributions to the anomaly prediction.
4. Generates structured, rule-based advisory recovery procedures for ground operators.

### Key Objectives
- **Data Generation:** Construct a physics-grounded synthetic telemetry dataset incorporating LEO orbital cycles (solar illumination, eclipse thermal decay, battery charge cycles) and non-trivial injected anomaly modes.
- **Preprocessing Pipeline:** Implement schema validation, domain-specific feature engineering (electrical power products, thermal differentials, nominal deviations), and standardized scaling.
- **Model Training & Architecture Extensibility:** Train Decision Tree, Support Vector Machine (SVM), and XGBoost on an identical stratified split utilizing an extensible `MODEL_REGISTRY` architecture.
- **Empirical Evaluation:** Compute actual Accuracy, Weighted Precision, Recall, F1-scores, confusion matrices, training times, and per-sample inference latencies.
- **Decision Support System:** Synthesize rule tables that provide operator guidance without generating autonomous spacecraft commands.

## 4. System Architecture
The SpaceGuard AI architecture is divided into four modular layers:

```
[ Telemetry Ingestion ] ---> [ Preprocessing & Feature Eng. ] ---> [ ML Classification Engine ] ---> [ Decision Support & UI ]
  • LEO Orbit Sim               • Schema Validation (11 feats)        • Decision Tree (CART)            • Feature Explainability
  • CSV Batch Upload            • Domain Physics (EPS, TCS)           • SVM (RBF Kernel)                • Rule Advisory Engine
  • Stream Buffer               • StandardScaler Normalization        • XGBoost (Softprob)              • SQLite Incident Store
```

### Layer 1: Telemetry Ingestion
Supports two input modalities:
- *Asynchronous LEO Orbit Simulator:* Runs on a background daemon thread, generating synthetic telemetry every 2.5 seconds with orbital phase progression and periodic anomaly injections.
- *REST API Upload:* Ingests batch CSV files via `/api/dataset/upload` or single-point telemetry via `/api/predict`.

### Layer 2: Preprocessing & Domain Feature Engineering
Validates the presence and data types of 11 base telemetry features. Deterministically computes 7 domain-guided features:
1. $P_{\text{EPS}} = V_{\text{bat}} \times I_{\text{bat}}$ (Electrical power delivered by battery)
2. $P_{\text{solar}} = V_{\text{solar}} \times I_{\text{solar}}$ (Instantaneous solar array generation)
3. $\Delta T_{\text{CPU}} = T_{\text{CPU}} - T_{\text{chassis}}$ (Thermal gradient between processor and structure)
4. $\Delta V_{\text{bat}} = |V_{\text{bat}} - 28.0|$ (Deviation from nominal 28.0V bus)
5. $\Delta T = |T_{\text{chassis}} - 22.0|$ (Deviation from nominal thermal baseline)
6. $\Delta P = |P_{\text{vessel}} - 101.3|$ (Deviation from avionics cabin nominal pressure)
7. $\Delta P_{\text{load}} = |P_{\text{load}} - 200.0|$ (Deviation from nominal power budget)

The resulting 18-dimensional feature vector is normalized using `StandardScaler` fitted strictly on the training partition.

### Layer 3: Machine Learning Engine
Implements an extensible `MODEL_REGISTRY` mapping model identifiers to scikit-learn and XGBoost estimators. Checkpoints are persisted in `.joblib` format.

### Layer 4: Decision Support & Persistence
Computes feature deviations, assigns severity ratings, logs events into an ACID-compliant SQLite database (`anomaly_history`), and serves dynamic visualizations to the web UI.

## 5. Dataset & Failure Modes

### Dataset Characteristics
- **Total Observations:** 7,200 records sampled at 10-second intervals (spanning 20.0 hours / ~13.3 LEO orbits).
- **Nominal vs. Anomaly Balance:** 4,977 Normal records (69.1%) and 2,223 Anomaly records (30.9%).
- **Severity Breakdown:** 4,977 Normal, 1,163 Warning, 1,060 Critical.

### Subsystem Failure Modes Modeled
1. **Temperature Spike (Thermal Anomaly):** Subsystem/payload thermal dissipation exceeds radiator capability ($T > 55^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{\text{CPU}} > 75^\circ\text{C}$).
2. **Battery Voltage Drop (Power Anomaly):** Battery cell internal short or depth-of-discharge sag ($V_{\text{bat}} < 23.0\text{V}$, $I_{\text{bat}} > 8.0\text{A}$).
3. **Current Surge (Electrical Anomaly):** Transient spike on power distribution unit branch ($I_{\text{bat}} > 10\text{A}$, $P_{\text{load}} > 350\text{W}$).
4. **Excessive Power Consumption (Payload Overload):** Continuous payload runaway or heater stuck-on ($P_{\text{load}} > 320\text{W}$).
5. **Sensor Value Drift (Instrument Malfunction):** Cumulative linear transducer drift on cabin pressure ($P_{\text{vessel}} < 75\text{ kPa}$).
6. **Communication Signal Drop (RF Anomaly):** Attenuation of downlink carrier ($RSSI < -100\text{ dBm}$, transponder lock loss).
7. **Multiple Simultaneous Abnormalities (Compound Anomaly):** Space weather or solar particle event causing simultaneous radiation influx, thermal escalation, and communication degradation.

## 7. Comparative Analysis & Discussion

### Accuracy vs. Complexity Trade-Off
- **SVM** achieved the highest overall accuracy (99.44%) and F1-score (99.44%). The non-linear RBF kernel mapped multi-sensor deviations into higher-dimensional space where complex compound anomalies (such as simultaneous solar radiation storm and voltage drop) formed cleanly separable boundaries.
- **XGBoost** achieved 99.03% accuracy with excellent balanced multi-class performance across all 8 classes. Its tree ensemble structure provided robust generalization against synthetic Gaussian sensor noise.
- **Decision Tree** achieved 98.12% accuracy. While slightly lower than ensemble/kernel methods due to orthogonal decision boundary constraints, it exhibited the fastest training time (0.103 s) and the lowest inference latency (0.0009 ms per record).

### Inference Latency for Real-Time Flight Systems
Onboard spacecraft flight software and ground station telemetry decom-processors operate under strict hard or soft real-time constraints. With an inference latency of **0.0009 ms to 0.0259 ms**, all three models easily satisfy telemetry ingestion cadences of 10 Hz (100 ms budget) or even 100 Hz (10 ms budget), proving their computational viability for operational deployment.

### Explainability & Decision Support Integration
Unlike black-box deep neural networks, the Decision Tree provides native feature importances. Feature attribution analysis identified deviations in `pressure_dev`, `temp_dev`, `v_bat_dev`, and `radiation_level` as the primary drivers of split entropy reduction. By pairing these scores with rule-based recovery checklists, ground controllers receive immediate, interpretable guidance during critical anomalies.

## 8. Conclusion & Future Scope

### Conclusion
This project developed and validated **SpaceGuard AI**, a full-stack academic simulation and decision-support platform for satellite telemetry anomaly classification. By evaluating Decision Tree, SVM, and XGBoost models on a physics-grounded synthetic telemetry dataset, we demonstrated that machine learning classifiers can achieve >98% multi-class accuracy with sub-millisecond latency while providing transparent feature attribution and operator decision support.

### Future Scope
1. **Unsupervised Outlier Detection:** Integration of Isolation Forests, One-Class SVMs, or Autoencoders for zero-day anomaly detection without labeled failure classes.
2. **Edge Deployment:** Quantization and compilation of trained estimators onto radiation-hardened spaceborne microprocessors (e.g., ARM Cortex-R or RISC-V space chips).
3. **NASA Public Telemetry Validation:** Validating the pipeline on archival telemetry from NASA missions (e.g., Kepler, SMAP, or Mars Science Laboratory).
